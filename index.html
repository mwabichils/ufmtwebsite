Fibroids and adenomyosis. There is a treatment that does not involve surgery.

HIFU treats uterine fibroids and adenomyosis without a cut, and the uterus stays where it is.

HIFU is in clinical use in more than 27 countries, and it is included in the global best practice guidance for treating fibroids published by FIGO, the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics.

What is HIFU treatment for fibroids?

Focused sound. Not a scalpel.

HIFU, or High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound, is a non-surgical treatment for uterine fibroids and adenomyosis. Focused ultrasound beams pass through the abdominal wall and converge inside the uterus, heating and destroying the target tissue without any incision. The uterus is preserved and most patients go home the same day.

Ultrasound beams pass harmlessly through your skin and meet at a single point inside the uterus. At that point, and only there, the tissue heats and the fibroid is destroyed. The healthy tissue around it is left alone.

Nothing is cut. There is no blade and no surgical wound. Your body absorbs the treated tissue gradually over the months that follow.

  • No cut, no scar. Nothing is cut and nothing is stitched.
  • Usually no general anaesthetic. Light sedation keeps you comfortable and relaxed.
  • Home the same day. Most suitable patients are back to normal activity within one to three days.
  • Your uterus stays. The treatment targets the fibroid, not the organ.

HIFU is not experimental. In 2025 it was included in the best practice guidance for the diagnosis and treatment of fibroids published by FIGO, the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics. It is now also available to private patients in South Africa.

Fibroid and adenomyosis symptoms

What fibroids and adenomyosis can feel like.

Fibroids and adenomyosis can cause heavy periods, pelvic pain, pressure or bloating, and the tiredness that often comes with heavy blood loss. Symptoms vary a lot from one person to the next. Some women have severe ones, and many have none at all.

Fibroids are common. By the age of 50, close to two thirds of women will have developed them. Among African women the figure reaches up to 80 per cent, and the fibroids are often larger and more numerous.

Surgery is a good and appropriate treatment for many women, and for some it remains the right one. But it has not always been the only option, and for suitable patients it no longer has to be the first one considered. HIFU sits alongside myomectomy, hysterectomy and embolisation as one more choice to discuss with your specialist.

Adenomyosis treatment without a hysterectomy

Understanding the impact of adenomyosis on daily life.

Adenomyosis is historically difficult to diagnose. HIFU offers an alternative treatment pathway to consider.

HIFU is another option to discuss with your doctor. It treats adenomyotic tissue without a cut and without removing the uterus.

In published research, women treated with HIFU for adenomyosis reported significant reductions in period pain, sustained over three to twelve months. Outcomes vary between patients, and HIFU does not suit everyone.

What happens during HIFU treatment

What to expect, step by step.

Four steps, from first message to follow-up.

  1. 01

    Get in touch

    Fill in the short form on this page. The team will get back to you to arrange a consultation and tell you what to bring. They handle bookings only — they cannot assess your scans or tell you whether HIFU will suit you.

  2. 02

    Consultation and imaging

    You see the specialist. Your symptoms, history and scans are reviewed, and imaging is used to map the affected tissue precisely. This is where suitability is decided, honestly and either way.

  3. 03

    Treatment day

    You arrive, you are made comfortable, and the treatment is delivered through the abdominal wall under imaging guidance. Most treatments take between one and four hours, depending on the size and number of fibroids. There is no cut and no stitch.

    Recovery and follow-up

    Most patients go home the same day. Some centres monitor overnight. Many suitable patients return to normal activity within one to three days. You are seen again for follow-up, and the treated tissue shrinks gradually over the months that follow.

HIFU is not suitable for every patient or every fibroid. Suitability is assessed by the treating clinician after imaging, and outcomes vary between patients.

HIFU, the uterus and future fertility

About fertility

HIFU leaves the uterus intact, which keeps future options open. It is not a fertility treatment and it does not promise pregnancy. If having children matters to you, raise it at your consultation so that it forms part of the decision.

HIFU for fibroids: frequently asked questions

Questions women ask us.

What is HIFU?

HIFU stands for High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound. It uses focused sound waves to treat tissue inside the body without making any cuts.

What conditions does it treat?

Mainly uterine fibroids. In some cases it is also used for adenomyosis.

What are fibroids?

Fibroids are non-cancerous growths in the womb. They can cause heavy bleeding, pelvic pressure, pain or bloating.

How does HIFU work?

The specialist directs focused ultrasound energy at the fibroid. The heat damages the fibroid tissue, and it shrinks over time.

Is HIFU surgery?

No. There are no cuts and no stitches.

How is HIFU different from uterine fibroid embolisation?

Embolisation blocks the blood supply to the fibroid, using a thin tube passed through an artery. HIFU destroys the fibroid tissue directly with focused sound, and nothing enters your body. Both preserve the uterus. Your specialist will explain which one suits your fibroids.

Will I need a general anaesthetic?

Usually not. Light sedation is often used so that you stay relaxed and comfortable during treatment.

How long does treatment take?

Usually one to four hours, depending on the size and number of fibroids.

Will I stay in hospital?

Many patients go home the same day. Some are monitored overnight and discharged the next day if all is well.

Does it hurt?

Most women feel some discomfort, warmth or cramping during or after treatment. It is usually manageable.

What side effects can happen?

Common ones are mild pain, cramping, temporary vaginal discharge, a low-grade fever, or skin changes over the treated area. Serious complications are rare.

Who may not be suitable?

HIFU is not right for everyone. It may not be recommended if you are pregnant, if a fibroid is very large or awkwardly positioned, if you have extensive abdominal scarring, if bowel lies in the treatment path, if you are significantly overweight, or if you have certain metal implants or other conditions that make treatment unsafe or less effective.

Will HIFU affect my fertility?

HIFU leaves the uterus intact. If having children in future is important to you, discuss it with the specialist at your consultation so that it forms part of the decision.

What happens after treatment?

You will need a short recovery period, and most women return to normal activities within a few days. The fibroid shrinks gradually after treatment. HIFU can sometimes be repeated if symptoms return.

How long before the fibroid shrinks?

Shrinking is gradual rather than immediate. Many women notice their symptoms improving in the first months, and the fibroid continues to reduce over the months that follow. How much it shrinks varies between patients.

What if my fibroids come back?

Fibroids can return after any treatment that leaves the uterus in place, including surgery. HIFU can sometimes be repeated, and because the uterus is intact, your other options remain open.

Why does adenomyosis take so long to diagnose?

Adenomyosis is harder to see on a scan than fibroids, and its symptoms overlap with several other conditions. Many women are told the pain is normal. A specialist assessment with the right imaging is usually what settles it.
